HOLLY BOT

Privacy Policy

Last updated 25 September 2026

Holly Bot is an AI assistant app where you create bots, chat with them and let them work for you, with Holly Bot's AI, which runs DeepSeek's AI models through OpenRouter. What you keep in Holly Bot is stored in your own account, which only you can open. There are no ads, no analytics and no tracking.

Your account and what it holds

Holly Bot requires an account. Everything the app keeps for you is stored in it: your bots and their settings, your chats and messages, your bots' memories, files you or your bots save or attach, routines, tasks between bots, activity logs, usage counts, your profile (the name and details you choose to share with your bots) and your settings, including the keys you add for other services. For your AI credits we also keep how many credits your plan gives you, how many are left this month, and how many requests and tokens this month's used.

That data is held for us by Convex, the hosting provider that runs our backend, in the United States. It is kept so that you can sign in on any device and find the same bots and chats. Each account can read and change only its own data; we enforce that on our server, not only in the app.

We use your data only to run the app for you. We do not read it, analyse it, sell it, or share it with anyone, and we do not use it to train AI models.

The app on your device also keeps your sign-in session and, until they reach our server, any changes made while you were offline. Signing out sends those changes and removes them from the device.

Payments and your subscription

Holly Bot is a paid subscription, and Stripe handles the payments. You enter your card or other payment details on Stripe's checkout page, and Stripe keeps them under its own privacy policy. We never see or store your full card number.

When you first subscribe, we give Stripe your account's email address and name, so it can bill you and send your receipts. From Stripe we keep your customer and subscription identifiers, your plan, whether you pay monthly or yearly, and your subscription's status and when it renews or ends, so Holly Bot knows it can open for you. Settings → Subscription opens Stripe's billing portal, where you can see your invoices, update your payment details or cancel.

Deleting your account also deletes your customer record at Stripe, which cancels your subscription straight away. Stripe keeps records of past payments as the law requires.

Your plan's computer

Each subscription comes with a dedicated server of your own at Vultr, a cloud provider, in Chicago in the United States. It runs Holly Computer, linked to your account, so your bots can work there around the clock with the same access to your account that the app has. Files your bots make on it, its web browser (including sites your bots sign in to) and plugins you add there are kept on that server rather than in your account. We don't look at what's on it, and it isn't backed up.

The app reaches it over an encrypted connection, with an address and a key that only your account is given. When you move to a smaller plan, those files are copied to your new server and the old one is deleted. When your subscription ends or your account is deleted, the server and everything on it is deleted.

Signing in

You sign in with Apple or with Google. We store the email address they give us, the name they share and, with Google, your profile picture address, along with an identifier for your Apple or Google account, so we can recognise you the next time you sign in and show who is signed in.

Sign in with Apple shares your name only the first time you use it. If you choose Apple's Hide My Email, we receive a relay address instead of your real one, and that is the address we store. Apple and Google handle sign-in under their own privacy terms.

Holly Bot's AI (OpenRouter)

When a bot works, the app on your device, or your plan's computer, sends what the bot needs to Holly Bot's AI: the conversation, the bot's instructions and relevant memories, your profile, and any files or images involved. It goes to our server, which passes it on to OpenRouter, a service that runs AI models from many companies, under Holly Bot's own OpenRouter account. OpenRouter hands it to one of the companies that run DeepSeek's models on its network and passes the answer back to your bot. We only let OpenRouter use companies that say they don't keep what they're sent or train AI on it, which leaves out DeepSeek's own service. Our server doesn't keep what's in these requests or answers, and we don't read them. For each request it keeps only how many tokens it used and what that came to, to count your AI credits.

OpenRouter and the company that runs each request receive it from Holly Bot, not from an account of yours, and handle it under their own privacy policies. OpenRouter is based in the United States, and so are most of the companies it uses. What they receive can include your name and other details from your profile and your chats, since they're part of what your bots work with.

Web search, reading pages and other tools

When a bot searches the web, the search is sent to the service you set up for it (Tavily, Exa or Jina) with your key. When a bot reads a web page, the page's address is sent to Jina's reader service to fetch it, or the page is fetched directly. The first time a bot runs Python code, the Python runtime is downloaded from the jsDelivr CDN; the code itself runs on your device. Icons shown beside the sources a bot cites are loaded from Google's website-icon service, which receives those websites' addresses.

Plugins (MCP servers) and other services you connect receive what a bot sends them while using them.

Gmail, Outlook and GitHub

You can connect your Gmail, Outlook or GitHub account in Settings → Plugins, so your bots can use it when you ask. Connecting takes you to Google, Microsoft or GitHub, which show what Holly Bot is asking for, and nothing is connected unless you agree there. You can also connect GitHub with an access token you make on GitHub.

What bots can do with them. With Gmail or Outlook, bots can search and read your email, send email from your address, and delete email. Deleted email goes to your Trash (Gmail) or Deleted Items (Outlook), where you can get it back, unless you ask for it to be deleted for good. For that, Holly Bot asks Google for full access to your Gmail, the only permission that allows deleting for good, and Microsoft for access to read and change your Outlook mail. Holly Bot doesn't change your mailbox settings. With GitHub, bots can list, create, change and delete your repositories and their files, and make the other GitHub requests you ask for. Bots do this only when you ask them to, or in routines you set up. With Auto-review on, a bot asks you before sending or deleting email, showing you exactly which emails, and before making a repository public. It always asks before deleting email for good or deleting a repository.

What we store. For each connection we store the account's email address or GitHub username, the permissions you granted, and the access tokens that let your bots use it. The tokens are encrypted on our server, never sent to your devices, and used only when one of your bots acts for you.

Where your email and code go. When a bot reads an email or a file, our server fetches it from Google, Microsoft or GitHub and passes it to the app, which sends it to Holly Bot's AI as part of the conversation (see Holly Bot's AI above). What a bot reads is kept only as part of your chats in your account. We don't keep a separate copy of your mailbox or repositories, we don't read them, and we don't use them for anything but the work you ask for.

Google user data. Holly Bot's use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. We use Gmail data only to provide the email features you use in Holly Bot. We transfer it only as needed to provide them (to Holly Bot's AI, through OpenRouter, at your direction), for security, or to comply with the law. We never use it for advertising, never sell it, and never use it to train AI models, and no person reads it unless you ask us to (for example for support), or where security or the law requires it.

Disconnecting. Disconnect a service at any time in Settings → Plugins. Its tokens are deleted, and Holly Bot asks Google or GitHub to take back its access. Microsoft offers no way to do that for a single app: the access simply stops being renewed, and you can also remove Holly Bot at account.microsoft.com. An access token you made on GitHub stays valid until you delete it there. Deleting your Holly Bot account disconnects everything the same way. You can also remove Holly Bot's access directly in your Google, Microsoft or GitHub account settings.

Holly Computer

Holly Computer is an optional companion you can run on your own PC or Mac, so your bots can work around the clock and use that computer with the permissions you give them. When you link it to your account, it keeps your bots, chats, memories, files and keys in your account, like the app does, and signs in with a session of its own that you can end from Settings. Anything it kept before is moved into your account, and its old data folder is set aside on the computer for you to delete. Until it's linked, it keeps them on the computer.

Your phone reaches it through a Cloudflare tunnel or your Wi-Fi network, protected by a pairing token that stays on your devices. Once it's linked, it also keeps in your account the address your devices can reach it at, an access key of its own for them, and when it last ran, so that Holly Bot on each device signed in to your account can connect to it. The access key stops working when you unlink the computer, and restarting Holly Computer with --new-token replaces it.

Voice

Dictation uses your device's or browser's speech recognition, which may send your voice to Apple or Google to turn it into text. Read-aloud uses the voices built into your device. Holly Bot does not record or store audio.

Notifications

Notifications, if you turn them on, are shown by the app on your device. We do not run a push notification service.

What we never do

We do not run analytics, telemetry or advertising. We do not sell or rent personal information or share it beyond what this policy describes, we load no third-party trackers, and we do not build a profile of you. The app itself is served by GitHub Pages, which, like any web host, processes the network information needed to deliver it.

Deleting your data

To remove your account and everything in it, email us at chris@mangomarketeers.com from the address you sign in with. We erase your bots, chats, memories, files, routines, settings, keys and AI credits, your connected Gmail, Outlook and GitHub accounts, and your sign-in details, and cancel your subscription. It cannot be undone.

To erase what's in your account but keep the account, open Settings, then Data & Backup, then Erase all data. Signing out deletes nothing; your account is there when you sign in again.

Deleting your account also unlinks any Holly Computer. A Holly Computer that was never linked keeps its bots on that computer; delete them there.

Children

Holly Bot is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect information from them. If you believe a child has created an account, contact us and we will delete it.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the date at the top of this page changes with it. Material changes will be noted in the app.
